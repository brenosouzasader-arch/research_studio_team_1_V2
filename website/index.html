The service

Share your roof's solar with the neighbours who can use it

Roofscapes (Re)Powered is for owners and managers of large flat roofs, such as offices and schools that are closed at weekends. You give a building. The service reads its roof, works out how many solar panels fit and how much power they make every hour of the year, and compares that with what the building itself uses. What is left over is spare power. The service then finds the group of nearby buildings that can use the most of it, tests whether a battery pays, and shows what the owner and the neighbours save. The owner pays for the panels and sells only the spare power, at a price below the grid's.

  1. Find the propertyaddress to building, from Catastro and LiDAR
  2. Measure the roofusable area and panel layouts
  3. Simulate the sunshade and weather, hour by hour
  4. Compare with usehow much the building uses, what is spare
  5. Match neighboursthe best group within reach
  6. Check the moneybattery, owner and neighbours

Step 01 · find the property

From an address to a building

Type an address, a street number or a cadastral reference. The service finds the building in the Spanish Cadastre (Catastro), takes its height and roof shape from LiDAR and checks whether the roof is flat. Below, the properties we have already run.

Where the buildings are

Where each footprint comes from

measured · Catastro, ICGC LiDAR

Catastro first, LiDAR when Catastro has nothing. Step 01 takes each building's footprint from the Spanish Cadastre (Catastro). Some plots, mostly public buildings such as schools and the fire station, have a cadastral reference but no building in Catastro's building service. For those, step 01 traces the outline from ICGC LiDAR: the points more than 6 m above the ground inside the plot. Year built, use and number of homes stay unknown for these buildings.

Checked again in step 02. Step 02 reads every building, host and participants alike, from both LiDAR and the aerial photo (ICGC orthophoto). Comparing the two with the step 01 footprint confirms that the Catastro outline is right, or shows where it is not.

How this layout was made

assumption · block rules
  1. Fit. Panel rows are laid on the usable roof, as many as fit.
  2. Shade. Panels that lose more than 40% of their sun to shade are dropped.
  3. Cut-off. The neat layout drops panels below 85% of the roof's best panel (weak).
  4. Blocks. Of the rest, only big enough rectangles are kept: at least 12 m² of panels, at least 2 panels wide both ways, whichever way they run. Good panels outside such a block are dropped (loose).
